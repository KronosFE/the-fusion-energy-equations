The Hardest Objections, Answered

The skeptic's questions elsewhere in this apparatus are the ones a curious reader asks. These are the ones a hostile expert asks — the sharpest technical objections to this program, stated as strongly as I can state them, and answered without flinching. If any of these has a knockout in it, I would rather you find it here, in my own voice, than discover I hid it.

"Tandem mirrors were abandoned for good reasons. The plug problem was never solved."

True, and I will not pretend otherwise. The open magnetic mirror was the great disappointment of twentieth-century fusion: the ends leaked, the tandem plugs never held a deep enough electrostatic barrier, and the field moved on to the tokamak for sound reasons. What changed is not that someone solved the old plug on the old terms. It is a single input: field. The rare-earth superconducting tape that makes a 26.49-tesla plug buildable at a small bore did not exist when mirrors were abandoned. That is why the old dead end is worth reopening — and why I am emphatic that reopening is not the same as closing. The plug-density requirement, a ratio near 16, is the design's single largest open physics gate, and the ~100 MW test burner around 2032 exists for the sole purpose of settling it in hardware. If it fails there, the burner fails. I have put that sentence in every chapter that touches the machine.

"You are betting the whole thing on lunar helium-3, which is science fiction."

The fleet economics — not the physics — depend on cheap helium-3, and terrestrial supply cannot provide it at fleet scale. That is the largest external dependency in the plan, and it is dated deliberately late (~2040) and drawn wide. Two honest mitigations, neither of which I will oversell: first, the early machines do not need it — the demonstrator and the first commercial units run on the helium-3 the breeder itself accrues, so the physics and the first hardware are provable without a single gram from the Moon. Second, if lunar supply slips or never arrives, the burner does not vanish; it stays a small, demonstrated fleet rather than a civilization-scale one. The Moon is the hinge on how big, not on whether it works.

"Your breeding ratio is marginal — and real blankets always underperform."

Correct that nothing is demonstrated until the FOAK measures it — that measurement is the demonstration gate. But the design’s stance is the opposite of an overpromise: the frozen lever runs 1.03–1.06 on the literal recipe and 1.18–1.20 with a dedicated solid-beryllium layer (OpenMC on ENDF/B-VIII.0, gate BR-L2-A7), and the machine is carried as tritium-marginal by design. We do not claim a surplus; we claim a lever, with the blanket parameters that move it stated in the open. If real blankets underperform the model, the lever is the mechanism by which the design absorbs it — and the FOAK’s measured TBR settles the question in hardware around 2030–31.

"High-efficiency direct energy conversion has never worked at scale."

Also true, and it is why the burner's headline closure does not depend on the ambitious part. The field has assumed 70–80 percent for direct conversion for decades; we book the measured single-stage ion-converter value, about 0.48, and let that carry the whole power balance. The exotic electron-channel recovery — the quasineutral expander — is carried as bounded upside, open-risk, with a pre-registered experiment, not as a load-bearing assumption. The closure at Q_E ≈ 1.318 stands on measured efficiencies. If the expander never works, the number barely moves.

We did not route around the hard questions. We wrote them down first, in their strongest form, and then answered them one at a time.

"A spherical-tokamak centrepost cannot survive the neutron flux."

It cannot survive indefinitely, and we do not ask it to. The breeder's centrepost is neutron-lifetime-limited, and it is designed from the outset as a scheduled-replacement component — a consumable part on a maintenance cadence, not a lifetime one. That is not a workaround; it is the honest consequence of putting a high-field magnet close to a 14 MeV source in a compact geometry. And confronting 14 MeV neutrons in materials is precisely the breeder's near-term business, which is materials, not electricity. The machine whose job is to qualify how components survive neutrons is the right machine to take that limit on directly.

"A design solved on paper means nothing. Show hardware."

Agreed, completely — and this is the distinction the whole book is built around. "Solved" is a closed, self-consistent, published, reproducible design. "Demonstrated" is hardware on a test stand with an ammeter. We have the first and say plainly we do not yet have the second. The value of the first is not that it is a result; it is that, for the first time, an independent physicist can regenerate the central numbers from open code and try to break them. That is a real and checkable thing. It is not a working reactor, and this book never calls it one.

"Publishing the full design gives away your advantage."

The advantage was never secrecy. A closed design that no one can check is indistinguishable, from the outside, from a bluff — and fusion has seen enough bluffs. The durable advantages are execution, a secured supply chain for a material almost no one makes at scale, the granted public patent that anchors the approach, and the years of practice the breeder buys before the burner fleet begins. Openness is not a leak; it is the credibility strategy. A design that has to hide cannot ask to be believed.

A claim you can check is worth more than a claim you must trust. Every hard number in this book was built to be attacked.

"The AI safety results are from a simulation, not a burning plasma."

Yes — every control and safety number in this book comes from a physics-based digital twin, from public data, or from simulation, and each is tagged with its provenance in the deposited record. Kronos operates no fusion plant. The three thousand caught violations, the forty-seven-millisecond warning, the certified safety envelope — all of it lives on the twin, not on a plasma. I state that in the control chapter and again here, because a safety claim that hides its own basis is worse than no claim at all.

"D–³He is not aneutronic. You still make neutrons."

Correct, and I say low-neutron, never zero-neutron, everywhere in this book. Deuterium ions fuse with one another, and one branch makes a neutron; at the burner's operating point that unavoidable channel carries about 5.44 percent of the fusion power — against roughly 80 percent for deuterium–tritium. That residual is a shielding problem, not a plant-destroying one, and it is the honest reason the burner can be worked near and converted directly. The claim is a factor-of-fifteen reduction in neutron burden, precisely stated — not the myth of a neutron-free reactor.

"Compact high-field machines just concentrate the neutron damage."

True — for a compact deuterium–tritium power reactor, a smaller volume means a fiercer neutron load per unit wall, and that is a real problem the high-field tokamak programs must answer. It is also exactly why the power machine in this program is not a compact D–T tokamak. The burner is a low-neutron D–³He mirror, and the only compact high-field D–T machine here is the breeder — whose entire purpose is to make neutrons and whose components are designed as replaceable parts. The architecture answers the objection by refusing to make firm power in a neutron bath in the first place.

---

If none of these landed a knockout, it is not because I softened them. It is because the honest answer to a hard objection is usually not "you are wrong" but "yes, and here is exactly how far that goes, and where it stops." That is the whole discipline of this book, compressed into ten hard questions.
